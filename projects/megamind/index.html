Project-wide objectives

Project Overview

MEGAMIND is a multidisciplinary research programme funded by the Dutch Research Council (NWO), bringing together universities, research institutes, grid operators and industry partners to investigate the digital transformation of distribution electricity systems.

The programme explores how artificial intelligence, data-driven technologies and appropriate regulatory frameworks can enable more autonomous and coordinated operation of local electricity systems. It addresses the interaction between distributed energy resources, network operation, market participants and regulatory arrangements.

Within this broader programme, my PhD research focused on the design and simulation of local electricity and flexibility markets, particularly the strategic behaviour of market participants under physical network constraints.

My technical contribution

My Research

I developed optimization and simulation approaches to investigate how local electricity market structures, bidding strategies and pricing mechanisms affect market outcomes.

A central part of my research was the development of a simulation environment in which market participants could learn strategic bidding behaviour through reinforcement learning. This enabled the comparison of alternative market designs and the evaluation of market power under different operating conditions.

The research covered several interconnected topics:

  • Local market architecture: Designing hierarchical market structures and network-constrained market-clearing mechanisms.
  • Strategic bidding: Modelling how prosumers and aggregators adapt their bidding strategies to maximize economic returns.
  • Multi-agent learning: Using multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) to simulate interacting market participants and assess the reliability of learned outcomes.
  • Market power: Investigating the applicability of market power detection indicators to distribution-level electricity markets.
  • Mechanism design: Evaluating how market-clearing and pricing rules can influence participant incentives and overall market efficiency.

Related research during my NCCR Automation Fellowship at Empa extended these interests to arbitrage across sequential local markets using hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning.
